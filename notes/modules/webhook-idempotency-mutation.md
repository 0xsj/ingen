# A webhook mutation should break idempotency across requests

A webhook contract becomes materially useful when a controlled repeated
delivery defect is observable and killed by the duplicate rule.

## Origin

This note came from extending the webhook-validation lab beyond its first
baseline-only slice.

## What

The `accepts-duplicate` fixture wraps the clean webhook subject and rewrites
only a duplicate response. The second delivery of the same event ID is
reported as a fresh `202` acceptance instead of the contracted `200` duplicate
response. The fixture provider exposes that variant to Sorna without claiming
to be a general source-level webhook provider.

## Why

Idempotency is a cross-request property. A single successful request test cannot
detect a subject that accepts the first delivery correctly but mishandles the
retry. Keeping the first mutation small makes the campaign result easy to
inspect before adding signatures, queues, or more provider machinery.

## Result

The clean baseline passed all 4 rules. The campaign produced 1 mutation, killed
it, and left 3 rules passing with `webhook.duplicate-is-idempotent` failing.
The measured identities from the fresh campaign workspace were:
